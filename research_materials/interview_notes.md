# Interview Notes — Vaguely Remembered Photo Retrieval

Method rehearsed: 30-minute remote session on the participant's own phone (screen share). Part A covers the critical incident,
using a recent retrieval the participant chose. Part B is think-aloud: the participant retries that retrieval live. Every
unsuccessful or effortful task is coded by its **first failing node** (D1 Remember · D2 Express · D3 Understand & match · D4 Evaluate ·
D5 Refine · D6 Available) and scored against hypotheses H1–H4 from `problemstatement.md`.

---

## SYN-P1 — Ananya, 29, product designer, Bengaluru
**Library:** ~22,000 items, Android, backup on since 2018. Searches "a few times a month". Screener outcome: effortful path.

**Task: "The small café we went to on our Goa trip"**
| | |
|---|---|
| Remembered | Goa trip with two friends, "end of the year, 2023 I think". Café was small, had a **blue door**, she had a cold coffee, and friend Rhea was in the photo. |
| Forgotten | Café name, exact dates, which of three phones took it (hers or Rhea's) |
| What she did | Typed `cafe goa` → 400+ results mixed with beach shacks and restaurants → scrolled ~2 min → typed `goa` and scrolled to Dec 2023 → opened 6 café-looking photos full screen, one by one |
| Where it broke | The target **was in the `cafe goa` results** (row 9), but thumbnails of three cafés looked alike and she scrolled past it. After the session, asked "anything distinctive in the picture?", she said "blue door — I didn't think search would get that." |
| Workaround | Searched her WhatsApp chat with Rhea for "photo", found a forwarded copy. 9 min total. |
| First failing node | **D2 Express** (withheld her most distinctive clue), then D4 Evaluate (scrolled past the target) |
| Hypotheses | H1 supported, H2 supported |

> "I type the thing I'd put on an album name, not the thing I actually remember."

## SYN-P2 — Ravi, 41, school teacher, Pune
**Library:** ~9,000 items, iPhone with Google Photos backup. Rarely searches. Screener outcome: gave up.

**Task: "The picture of the medicine I took when I was sick last year"**
| | |
|---|---|

| Forgotten | Brand name, exact month |
| What he did | `medicine` → 11 results, none right → `tablet` → photos of a Samsung tablet → scrolled July–Aug 2024 (~4 min) |

| First failing node | **D6 Available.** No retrieval change in Google Photos would have helped. |
| Hypotheses | Disconfirming case: the retrieval problem was really a capture/backup problem |

> "I assumed Google Photos has everything from my phone."

**Task 1: "Page 2 of my rent agreement"** **Found in 20 s.** Typed `rent agreement`, and the text in the photo matched. Quick-success contrast: the words she remembered were printed in the image.

**Task 2: "Whiteboard from a workshop — the one with the funnel diagram"**

## SYN-P5 — Farhan, 24, postgraduate student, Hyderabad
**Library:** ~48,000 items, over half screenshots. Searches daily. Screener outcome: gave up.

**Task: "The screenshot of a meme about exam season a friend sent in March"**
| | |
|---|---|
| Remembered | Gist of the joke ("something like 'the syllabus vs the time I have'"), a **cartoon cat**, black background |

## Synthesis (n = 6 participants, 7 tasks)

**Outcome of the remembered task:** 1 quick success (P3 T1). 5 found only with effort or outside Photos (P1, P3 T2, P4, P6, and P5 via a friend). 1 not in library (P2).

**First failing node across the 6 effortful or failed tasks**
| Node | Tasks | Participants |
|---|---|---|
| D2 Express | 2 | P1, P4 |
| D5 Refine | 2 | P3, P6 |
| D3 Understand & match | 1 | P5 |
| D6 Available | 1 | P2 |
| D4 Evaluate | 0 as first node, 1 secondary | P1 |
| D1 Remember | 0 | none. Every participant held at least one distinctive clue. |

**Patterns (a pattern needs 3 or more participants)**
1. **People hold distinctive clues but query with generic labels** (P1, P4, P6; partly P3). Blue door, car-shaped cake, marigold garland, funnel diagram. What they typed was the category and the place.
   Reason given: they don't believe search understands descriptions ("search is for names").
2. **A failed query gives no signal about which clue missed** (P3, P5, P6). Recovery is guesswork: rephrase, change the date, or brute-force scroll.
3. **Confident-but-wrong anchors are fatal** (P3; partly P1's "end of 2023"). Date and year are used because they are easy to apply, and once applied they silently exclude the target.
4. **Workarounds leave the product** (P1, P2, P5, and P3's colleague): WhatsApp chats and asking friends or family.

**Hypothesis scorecard**
| H | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| H1 Expression gap | Supported (4 of 6) | P1, P4, P6, P3 withheld their distinctive clue; P5 is the counter-case |
| H2 Recognition gap | Weak (1 of 6) | Only P1 scrolled past the target; the others recognised it instantly |
| H3 Coarse time scoping | Supported, narrow (2 of 6) | P3 (wrong year), P1 (approximate) |
| H4 Unguided recovery | Supported (3 of 6) | P3, P5, P6 |

**Candidate root cause (to be validated):** Users remember photos as **distinctive details plus a loose context**, but
they translate that memory into the **coarsest label they think search accepts**. When a query fails, the product gives **no
signal about which clue failed**, so users drop their best clues and fall back to scrolling or leaving the app.

**Where intelligence is needed (from these tasks):** eliciting the clue the user has but didn't type (D2), and explaining which
clue matched or missed so they can recover (D5). Recognition (D4) did not need help for most participants.
